# SOTA Architectures for GO Term / Protein Function Prediction

## Protein language model (PLM) embedding-based methods: how do systems use ESM-2/ESM-3, ProtT5/ProstT5 (etc.) embeddings for downstream GO classification?

### Takeaway
The field converged on a common pattern by ~2022–2024: take per-residue or mean-pooled embeddings from a large pretrained PLM (ESM-1b/ESM-2, ProtT5/ProtTrans) as a fixed or lightly-adapted feature extractor, then train a comparatively small downstream head (MLP, attention-pooling layer, or triplet network) to map embeddings to GO terms. This displaced hand-engineered features (BLAST hits, InterPro domains, k-mers) as the dominant single feature source, though the strongest systems still combine PLM embeddings with those older signals rather than replacing them outright.

### Cited Findings
- **NetGO 3.0 / LR-ESM**: replaces the prior Seq-RNN component of NetGO 2.0 with `LR-ESM`, which encodes each protein with ESM-1b (1280-dim per-residue embeddings), applies average pooling across positions to get a protein-level embedding, and feeds it to a logistic-regression-style component classifier that is combined via ensembling (a protein-language-model component is just one of several component classifiers) — [NetGO 3.0](https://academic.oup.com/gpb/article/21/2/349/7585485); [xlab PDF](https://xlab.sjtu.edu.cn/pdf/2023-GPB-NetGO3.0.pdf).
- **GOLabeler** (CAFA3 top performer, predecessor to NetGO) combines five component classifiers from different feature types — Naive (term frequency), BLAST-KNN, LR-3mer (sequence k-mers), LR-InterPro (domains/motifs), LR-ProFET (biophysical properties) — fused via a Learning-to-Rank (LTR) framework, predating PLM embeddings entirely — [GOLabeler](https://scite.ai/reports/golabeler-improving-sequence-based-large-scale-protein-68jx4gM).
- **ATGO**: uses ESM-1b as a frozen feature extractor, then trains a supervised triplet neural network on the multi-layer transformer embeddings, explicitly designed to widen the margin between positive and negative GO-term assignments; the composite **ATGO+** adds a sequence-alignment-based model on top for improved coverage — [ATGO PLOS Comp Bio](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1010793).
- **SPROF-GO**: extracts an initial sequence embedding matrix from **ProtTrans**, splits processing into two parallel MLPs — one produces a per-residue attention vector, the other a hidden embedding matrix — combines them via self-attention pooling into a single protein-level vector, then an MLP predicts GO-term probabilities; adds a homology-based **label diffusion** post-processing step and a hierarchical-learning-strategy step for consistency. Authors report it "surpasses state-of-the-art sequence-based and even network-based approaches by more than 14.5%, 27.3%, and 10.1% in AUPR" on the three sub-ontologies (self-reported), and claims good generalization to non-homologous proteins and unseen species — [SPROF-GO, Briefings in Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/24/3/bbad117/7085635); [GitHub](https://github.com/biomed-AI/SPROF-GO).
- **TALE**: replaces CNNs with a self-attention transformer over the raw sequence and jointly learns a **label embedding** for GO terms alongside the sequence embedding, using their joint similarity (rather than a plain classifier head) to score each term for each protein — intended specifically to generalize to "tail" (rarely-annotated) labels and to low-homology/novel-species proteins. `TALE-cmap` extends this to also ingest contact-map-derived structural embeddings — [TALE Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/37/18/2825/6182677); [GitHub](https://github.com/Shen-Lab/TALE).
- **PANDA2**: uses transformer-based PLM features as node/protein features combined with a **graph neural network that models the GO-DAG topology itself** (not just a downstream classifier on flat labels) — [PANDA2, NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/4/1/lqac004/6520105).
- **DeepGOZero**: is fundamentally sequence-based (an MLP/CNN-style encoder over sequence features) but its main innovation is combining the prediction loss with **four "normal form" losses derived from EL Embeddings**, an ontology-embedding technique that respects GO's Description Logic axioms; the sequence encoder itself is comparatively simple, and the paper reports best results when combined with `DiamondScore` (sequence-similarity-based scoring) as **DeepGOZero+** — [DeepGOZero, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/Supplement_1/i238/6617515); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9235501/).
- **DeepGOPlus**: does *not* use a modern PLM at all — it one-hot encodes sequence (21 × 2000) and runs it through multi-width 1D CNN filters (widths 8–128, 512 filters each, max-pooled), then combines the CNN output with a separate DiamondScore (sequence-similarity) prediction. Authors found the CNN filters learn motifs matching ~half of the InterPro annotations in the test set and resemble Pfam seed sequences, i.e., the network re-discovers domain-level signal from scratch. Self-reported Fmax of 0.390 (BPO), 0.557 (MFO), 0.614 (CCO) under CAFA3 evaluation, which the authors say would have ranked 2nd–3rd against actual CAFA3 submissions — [DeepGOPlus, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/36/2/422/5539866).
- **PROTGOAT** (CAFA5, ranked 4th of ~1600 methods per author self-report): an ensemble built from embeddings of **multiple diverse pretrained PLMs** plus features mined from **published literature text** about the protein — [PROTGOAT, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.01.587572v1.full).
- **ProtBoost** (CAFA5, self-reported 2nd place, score 0.58240): combines Prot-T5 PLM embedding features with one-hot taxon (species) features, fed into **Py-Boost** — described as the first gradient-boosting method able to predict thousands of targets simultaneously — with results further refined via **GNN-based stacking** across many individual models — [ProtBoost, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529); [GitHub](https://github.com/btbpanda/CAFA5-protein-function-prediction-2nd-place).
- Field-wide framing: "the strong performance of the protein language model (pLM) embedding baseline shows how the development of protein models outside the function prediction space can help support the development of function predictors" — [CAFA5 assessment paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142333/).
- ESM-2 is trained purely via masked-language-modeling on sequence and has no explicit structural supervision, whereas **ESM-3** (a 98B-parameter model from EvolutionaryScale) is explicitly multimodal, jointly modeling sequence, structure, and function tokens — [arXiv 2510.27040 (GeoPep)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27040).

### Inferences
- The trajectory across systems (GOLabeler → NetGO → NetGO 3.0; DeepGOPlus → DeepGOZero → ATGO/SPROF-GO/TALE) shows a consistent substitution: hand-engineered sequence features (k-mers, domain hits, alignment scores) are being replaced or supplemented by PLM embeddings as one input stream, but virtually no top system relies on PLM embeddings *alone* — sequence-similarity scoring (Diamond/BLAST) and/or ensembling with non-PLM signals persistently adds measurable value on top, suggesting PLM embeddings capture different (not strictly superordinate) information relative to homology search.
- CAFA5's top self-reported systems (ProtBoost, PROTGOAT, GOCurator — see ensembling section) all use PLM embeddings as *one component feature stream* inside a larger ensemble/boosting/stacking pipeline rather than as a single end-to-end classifier, reinforcing that in practice "PLM embeddings + classifier" alone is not what wins CAFA-style competitions at the very top of the leaderboard — it is table stakes, with ensembling providing the additional edge (see the dedicated ensembling section below).

### Gaps
- Could not obtain a report of DeepGOZero's raw sequence encoder architecture in detail (whether CNN- or MLP-based) beyond that it's simpler than DeepGOPlus's CNN; the fetched sources described the axiom-embedding side much more than the underlying sequence encoder.
- No source specifically detailed how ProstT5 (the structure-aware sequence-to-3Di variant of ProtT5) is used by any of the named systems above; searches surfaced ProtT5/ProtTrans usage (SPROF-GO, ProtBoost) but not ProstT5-specific GO-prediction systems within the tool budget.

## Structure-based methods: how is 3D structure incorporated, and does it empirically beat sequence-only PLM embeddings?

### Takeaway
Structure-aware methods (DeepFRI's contact-map GCN, GAT-GO, PANDA-3D on AlphaFold models) incorporate structure via graph representations built from residue contact/distance information, processed with GCNs or graph-attention/geometric-vector-perceptron networks; the empirical evidence is mixed-to-modest — some benchmarks find structure adds only "modest" gains over sequence-only PLM baselines and is sometimes matched or exceeded by pure-sequence ontology-aware methods, while newer work combining AlphaFold-predicted structure with PLM embeddings (PANDA-3D) reports outperforming both structure-only and sequence-only priors.

### Cited Findings
- **DeepFRI**: a two-stage architecture — stage 1 extracts residue-level features from sequence using an LSTM-based self-supervised language model; stage 2 builds a **contact map** from the 3D structure (an N×N binary matrix, with artificial local-connectivity contacts added for k=2 nearest sequence neighbors) and merges it with the LSTM features via **graph convolutional network (GCN)** layers to produce protein-level features predicting GO terms (BP/MF/CC) and EC numbers — [DeepFRI, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-23303-9).
- **Metagenomic-DeepFRI**: a follow-on pipeline scaling DeepFRI by searching/aligning contact maps for large metagenomic protein sets before running DeepFRI's GCN, addressing the cost of generating real structures at scale — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.27.720983v1.full); [GitHub](https://github.com/bioinf-mcb/Metagenomic-DeepFRI).
- **PANDA-3D**: combines Geometric Vector Perceptron GNN (GVP-GNN) layers over AlphaFold-predicted 3D structure with variant transformer decoder layers and ESM sequence embeddings for multi-label GO classification. Authors' own permutation-feature-importance analysis found "ESMs, tokens of sequence, and dihedrals" as the top three most important input features (sequence-derived features, notably, rank alongside structural ones). Self-reported: PANDA-3D "achieved noticeably better performance than an existing method that takes experimentally determined structures as input" and either outperformed or was comparable to several sequence/LM-based SOTA methods — [PANDA-3D, NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/6/3/lqae094/7728018); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11302463/).
- Independent 14-method benchmark study: on the biological-process (BP) domain, structure/PPI-integrating **DeepGraphGO** reached Fmax 0.427 vs. pure-sequence **DeepGOCNN** at 0.323 — but pure-sequence **DeepGOZero+** (ontology-aware, no structure) reached Fmax 0.463 for BP, "often matching or exceeding" structure-based approaches; the same benchmark states DeepFRI and GAT-GO (both structure-based) "added modest improvements but required predicted structures from AlphaFold2" — [comprehensive benchmark, PMC10883809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10883809/).
- A cross-attention structure/sequence-integration paper (PST) is reported to "consistently outperform... ESM-2, setting a new benchmark in protein function prediction," per a secondary summary of the "Endowing Protein Language Models with Structural Knowledge" line of work — [arXiv 2401.14819 summary via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14819).
- ESM-3 (EvolutionaryScale, 98B params) is explicitly trained across sequence, structure, and function modalities jointly, rather than adding structure on top of a sequence-only model post hoc, differentiating its approach from DeepFRI/PANDA-3D style late-stage structure grafting — [arXiv 2510.27040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27040).

### Inferences
- The empirical picture is genuinely mixed rather than a clean "structure wins": DeepFRI/GAT-GO's gains are frequently characterized in independent benchmarks as "modest," and at least one direct Fmax comparison (DeepGraphGO vs. DeepGOZero+ on BP) shows a pure-sequence, ontology-aware method beating a structure/network-integrating one. This suggests structural signal is not reliably additive over a *well-designed* sequence method, and much of the earlier "structure helps" narrative may reflect comparisons against weaker sequence-only baselines (e.g., plain DeepGOCNN) rather than against best-in-class PLM+ontology methods.
- The strongest structure-based result found (PANDA-3D) is explicitly a **hybrid** — AlphaFold structure GNN plus ESM sequence embeddings plus dihedral features — not structure alone, and its own feature-importance analysis ranks sequence-derived (ESM) features among the top three, which weakens any claim of structure being independently the dominant signal even in that system.
- Structure-based methods carry a real practical cost (dependence on real or AlphaFold-predicted structures, contact-map computation, GCN/GVP training) that pure-sequence PLM approaches avoid — a cost/benefit tradeoff not obviously justified given the "modest" gains reported.

### Gaps
- Could not find a CAFA5-specific (as opposed to independent-benchmark) head-to-head Fmax comparison isolating structure-based vs. sequence-only PLM submissions; the CAFA5 assessment paper full text could not be fully retrieved (persistent HTTP 429 rate-limiting from bioRxiv across three attempts), so any CAFA5-specific quantitative statement about structure vs. sequence is not confirmed from the primary assessment source.
- No source directly quantified ProstT5 (structure-aware sequence encoding via 3Di tokens) performance vs. plain ProtT5/ESM-2 for GO prediction specifically.

## GO-hierarchy-aware modeling: enforcing/exploiting the DAG and true-path rule — empirical benefit over flat classification?

### Takeaway
Hierarchy-aware approaches range from post-hoc propagation (ensure ancestor terms are predicted whenever a descendant is) to loss-level constraints (TALE's ancestor-must-score-higher-than-descendant constraint) to embedding the ontology's formal Description Logic axioms directly into training (DeepGOZero's EL Embeddings-based normal-form losses); independent benchmarking found ontology-aware methods can substantially outperform flat classifiers, especially on rare/low-annotation terms, though the size of the gap is metric- and ontology-branch-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- **DeepGOZero**'s core mechanism: computes standard binary cross-entropy between predictions and labels, but jointly optimizes this alongside **four "normal form" losses** adapted from **EL Embeddings**, which encode GO's Description Logic axioms (e.g., "regulation of binding is defined as biological regulation that regulates some binding") as geometric constraints in embedding space — enabling **zero-shot prediction** of GO terms that have zero or very few (<100) experimentally annotated training proteins, since GO has ~12,000 definition axioms and 500,000+ other axioms that can transfer information to poorly-annotated terms via their formal relationships to well-annotated ones — [DeepGOZero, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/Supplement_1/i238/6617515).
- **TALE**'s mechanism: jointly learns embeddings for sequence *and* GO labels, and (per the independent benchmark) uses "constrained loss functions ensuring ancestor nodes scored higher than child nodes," which the benchmark credits with "superior performance on depth-weighted metrics" — [comprehensive benchmark, PMC10883809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10883809/); [TALE, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/37/18/2825/6182677).
- **PANDA2**: uses a GNN specifically to model the topology of the GO-DAG itself (rather than treating GO terms as an unstructured flat label set), combined with PLM-derived protein features — [PANDA2, NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/4/1/lqac004/6520105).
- Quantitative flat-vs-ontology-aware comparison from the independent 14-method benchmark: on molecular function (MF) AUPR, ontology-aware **DeepGOZero+** scored 0.618 vs. flat **DeepGOCNN** at 0.302 — roughly double — [comprehensive benchmark, PMC10883809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10883809/). *(Note: this compares across methods that also differ in encoder architecture and sequence-similarity augmentation, not a controlled ablation isolating the ontology-loss term alone — treat the magnitude as suggestive rather than a clean causal estimate.)*
- General HMC literature background: the True Path Rule (and its "weighted" extension, wTPR) requires that if a child term is predicted, all its ancestor terms must also be predicted/consistent; bottom-up cumulative-probability propagation is a standard way to enforce this and "removes inconsistencies in the prediction" — [general HMC review sources, non-CAFA-specific](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-02326-2_24).
- Rare-term-specific finding: DeepGOZero "performs best for classes with very few annotations and results in a strong predictive signal in zero-shot predictions" and DeepGOZero+ (combined with DiamondScore) shows "significantly enhanced" performance versus DeepGOZero alone, "indicating close correlation between sequence similarity and functions" — i.e., ontology-axiom benefit and homology-based signal are complementary, not substitutes — [ResearchGate summary of DeepGOZero](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361592084_DeepGOZero_improving_protein_function_prediction_from_sequence_and_zero-shot_learning_based_on_ontology_axioms).
- A separate zero-shot-focused method, **ProTranslator** (zero-shot protein function prediction via textual GO-term descriptions rather than DL axioms), reportedly shows "substantial improvement over DeepGOPlus when each GO term only has a limited number of annotation proteins between 1 to 20, with improvement larger for terms that have fewer annotated proteins" — a different (text-embedding-based) route to the same rare-term problem DeepGOZero targets via formal axioms — [arXiv 2204.10286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.10286).
- NetGO's ensemble pipeline includes a **bottom-up propagation strategy along the GO DAG** as a final recalibration step on the combined probability score — [search-summarized description, primary source not directly confirmed by name in this pass].

### Inferences
- The benefit of ontology-awareness is concentrated specifically at the tail of the label distribution (rare/low-annotation GO terms), which is exactly the regime CAFA cares most about since specific/informative terms tend to be rarer; on well-annotated, shallow terms the gap between hierarchy-aware and flat methods is likely much smaller (not directly quantified in sources found, but consistent with why DeepGOZero's advantage is explicitly framed around zero/few-shot terms).
- Two philosophically different technical routes converge on similar rare-term gains: DeepGOZero's approach is intensional (uses the *formal logical definition* of a GO term to transfer knowledge from defining components), while ProTranslator's is extensional/distributional (uses the *natural-language description* of the term via text embeddings). This suggests the rare-term problem is tractable via multiple independent knowledge sources beyond raw training examples, and a system combining both (which no source described directly) might compound the gains.

### Gaps
- Could not find a controlled ablation (same base encoder, ontology loss on vs. off) isolating the pure effect size of ontology-aware losses independent of confounding architecture/data differences — all comparisons found are across genuinely different systems.
- Could not confirm the primary source describing NetGO's exact bottom-up GO-DAG propagation mechanism (only a secondary aggregator description was available); flagged as unconfirmed above.

## Ensembling and multi-model combination strategies in top CAFA-performing systems

### Takeaway
Every CAFA round's top-ranked system so far has been an ensemble/stacking pipeline combining multiple heterogeneous predictors (sequence, PLM embeddings, network/PPI data, and increasingly literature-mined text), rather than a single end-to-end model; authors and independent assessors consistently attribute a meaningful share of top-rank performance specifically to the combination/ensembling step rather than to any one component classifier being individually dominant.

### Cited Findings
- **GOLabeler** (CAFA3 top performer): an ensemble of 5 component classifiers (Naive, BLAST-KNN, LR-3mer, LR-InterPro, LR-ProFET) combined via a **Learning-to-Rank (LTR)** framework — the authors and secondary sources credit the combination of "6 different data sources" (feature families) as central to its CAFA3-winning performance, not any single component — [GOLabeler](https://scite.ai/reports/golabeler-improving-sequence-based-large-scale-protein-68jx4gM).
- **INGA** (another CAFA3 top method): builds a "consensus among predictors," combining PPI-network signal (STRING), sequence similarity (BLAST), and domain assignment (Pfam), with the consensus score explicitly tuned to maximize F-score — [search-summarized, primary source not independently confirmed in this pass].
- **NetGO → NetGO 2.0 → NetGO 3.0**: NetGO 2.0 was the top CAFA4 performer; NetGO 3.0 extends the ensemble by swapping in an ESM-1b-based component (`LR-ESM`) for the prior RNN-based sequence component, while keeping the overall multi-component ensembling architecture — each component method is separately calibrated against a benchmark set (estimated precision as a "confidence" weight), and final scores are recalibrated via bottom-up GO-DAG propagation — [NetGO 3.0, GPB](https://academic.oup.com/gpb/article/21/2/349/7585485).
- **GOCurator** (self-reported CAFA5 #1, team led by Prof. Shanfeng Zhu, Fudan University — same lab behind NetGO, also top-CAFA3/CAFA4 performer with GOLabeler/NetGO lineage): combines text mining, protein language models, protein structural information, and **GORetriever** (a deep-learning literature-to-GO-term matcher) into a diverse ensemble of multiple constructed models — [search-summarized description; primary paper not independently fetched in this pass — treat with some caution pending direct verification].
  - **GORetriever** specifically: a two-stage system — stage 1 retrieves candidate GO terms via proteins with similar textual descriptions; stage 2 reranks candidates by semantic matching between GO term definitions and the target protein's literature/description text — [GORetriever, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/Supplement_2/ii53/7749084).
- **ProtBoost** (self-reported CAFA5 #2, score 0.58240 out of >1600 participating methods): Prot-T5 embeddings + taxon features → **Py-Boost** gradient boosting (first GB method able to predict thousands of targets simultaneously) → further **GNN-based stacking of many individual models** as a final combination layer — the authors explicitly attribute a significant performance boost to this final stacking step ("The GCN-based approach performs stacking of many individual models and boosts the performance significantly") — [ProtBoost, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04529).
- **PROTGOAT** (self-reported CAFA5 #4): an ensemble of embeddings from **multiple diverse pretrained PLMs** plus literature-text features — [PROTGOAT, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.01.587572v1.full).
- Field-level pattern noted independently: "Top-performing methods tend to use a combination of different data sources and not only the amino acid sequence... demonstrating that ensemble approaches outperformed single models" (framed around GOLabeler/CAFA3, but consistent with the CAFA5 pattern above) — [search-summarized characterization of the CAFA3 evaluation paper line].

### Inferences
- There is a durable, multi-generation lineage of ensembling dominance at the very top of CAFA: the same Fudan/Zhu-lab methodology (GOLabeler → NetGO → NetGO 2.0 → GOCurator) has placed at or near the top across CAFA3, CAFA4, and CAFA5 by *layering new component predictors onto an existing ensemble framework* (swap in ESM embeddings, add GORetriever literature mining) rather than replacing the ensemble architecture itself — suggesting the ensembling/combination layer itself, not any single underlying model family, is the more durable source of competitive advantage in this specific benchmark.
- Among CAFA5's top self-reported finishers (GOCurator, ProtBoost, PROTGOAT), all three independently converged on **literature/text-mined features as a component**, alongside PLM embeddings — this is a comparatively new signal source (not present in CAFA3-era GOLabeler/INGA) that appears to have become a competitive necessity at the current SOTA frontier, not just an optional extra.

### Gaps
- Could not obtain a quantified ablation of "ensemble vs. best single component" performance gap for GOCurator, ProtBoost, or PROTGOAT specifically (e.g., how much Fmax the stacking/boosting step adds over the best individual PLM-based component) — sources describe the architecture and qualitatively assert the combination step "boosts performance significantly" (ProtBoost) but no source gave a specific numeric ablation.
- GOCurator's technical description above is built from secondary/aggregator search summaries rather than a directly fetched primary paper (the CAFA5 assessment paper itself could not be fully retrieved due to persistent bioRxiv rate-limiting) — treat the specific architecture claims about GOCurator as moderately, not fully, confirmed.
- Could not independently verify official final CAFA5 team rankings/scores from the CAFA organizers' own assessment paper (as opposed to self-reported "ranked 2nd"/"4th" claims by the ProtBoost and PROTGOAT authors themselves) — these self-reported placements should be flagged as such in the final report per the task's own instruction to distinguish self-reported vs. independently-assessed claims.

## Known open problems / weaknesses across all technique families

### Takeaway
Across PLM-embedding, structure-based, ontology-aware, and ensemble methods alike, the field's most persistent unresolved problems are: (1) sharply degraded accuracy on rare/low-annotation GO terms despite these being the most biologically informative; (2) a newly-identified "partial-knowledge" gap — predicting *new* annotations on already-partly-characterized proteins is markedly harder than predicting first annotations on totally novel proteins, and current methods are not optimized for it; (3) computational cost of the largest PLMs limiting practical fine-tuning/deployment; (4) evaluation/data-leakage subtleties (literature-mined annotations can postdate the nominal prediction cutoff); and (5) an general lack of discussion in the literature located here of formal probability calibration for GO prediction scores specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **Rare/novel term prediction**: "It is harder to predict rare (low-frequency) GO terms than common GO terms... but it is important to predict rare GO terms because they are usually specific and highly informative" — and roughly 20,000 terms each in MF, BP, and CC have fewer than 100 experimentally annotated proteins — [search-summarized characterization; consistent with DeepGOZero's own stated motivation](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/Supplement_1/i238/6617515). Independent benchmark: "GO terms with <30 annotations showed markedly reduced accuracy across all methods" tested, including modern ontology-aware and ensemble methods — [comprehensive benchmark, PMC10883809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10883809/).
- **The CAFA5 "partial-knowledge" (PK) problem**: CAFA5 introduced a new evaluation setting for predicting new annotations on proteins that already have *some* prior GO annotations (this setting accounts for ~69.7% of accumulated annotations in the evaluation window — i.e., it is the majority case in the real world, not a niche edge case). Performance dropped sharply relative to no-knowledge (NK) and limited-knowledge (LK) settings: **55.6%, 47.8%, and 59.7% decreases in Fmax_μ** for biological process, cellular component, and molecular function respectively, comparing NK to PK. The assessors attribute this partly to PK terms tending to be "deeper in the ontology" (harder) and partly to the fact that "model developers may not have optimized for this new setting" — i.e., this is at least partially an artifact of the field not yet targeting the problem, not solely an intrinsic difficulty ceiling — [CAFA5 assessment paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142333/).
- **No-knowledge (NK) vs. limited-knowledge (LK) generalization**: CAFA5 found performance "similar" between NK and LK settings, and reported "measurable gains over previous CAFA challenges, especially in the no-knowledge and limited-knowledge settings" — i.e., generalization to genuinely novel proteins has specifically improved release-over-release, in contrast to the newly-exposed PK weakness above — [CAFA5 assessment paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142333/).
- **Evaluation/data-leakage subtlety**: the CAFA5 assessors found "5.3% of annotations added to UniProt since t0 had publications corresponding to dates after t0" — a concrete, quantified leakage/timing risk specifically relevant to any literature-mining-based method (GOCurator's GORetriever, PROTGOAT's literature features) — [CAFA5 assessment paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142333/).
- **Incomplete ground truth as a structural limit** (not fixable by better modeling): "what is known about protein function will always be incomplete as testing for all functions, under all contexts, for all proteins is not feasible" — the assessors explicitly frame this as a ceiling on how good any evaluation-against-ground-truth score can ever be interpreted to mean, since "performance may change substantially between repeated evaluations, until possibly converging over time" — [CAFA5 assessment paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142333/).
- **Computational cost of large PLMs**: ESM-2 15B "cannot be fine-tuned even with high-memory GPUs such as the NVidia H100... using parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA," per a review of PLM inference/training efficiency; inference on long sequences with large PLMs can trigger memory errors — [Efficient inference, training, and fine-tuning of protein language models, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12481099/). Mitigations found: FlashAttention + sequence packing achieved "4–9× faster inference and 3–14× lower memory usage" (9.4× for ESM2, 3.8× for ESM-C specifically), and 4-bit quantization further reduces memory 2–3× "while preserving accuracy for missense variant effect prediction" (note: this specific accuracy-preservation claim is for variant-effect prediction, not GO function prediction, per the source) — [same source].
- **Medium vs. large PLM tradeoff**: "Medium-sized models, such as ESM-2 650M and ESM C 600M, demonstrated consistently good performance, falling only slightly behind their larger counterparts—ESM-2 15B and ESM C 6B—despite being many times smaller," per a transfer-learning benchmark on realistic datasets — suggesting diminishing returns from scaling PLM size specifically for downstream transfer tasks like function prediction — [Medium-sized protein language models, Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-05674-x).
- **Cross-species/taxonomically-distant generalization**: on proteins with ≤60% sequence identity to training data, structure/network-integrating methods like DeepGraphGO showed smaller performance decline than pure-sequence competitors when target-species proteins were excluded from training, suggesting PPI-network integration specifically helps cross-species transfer more than pure-sequence approaches do in that comparison — though this is a single independent-benchmark finding, not a CAFA-assessed result — [comprehensive benchmark, PMC10883809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10883809/).
- **General imbalance/shared-representation tension**: "reducing feature interference between shared sequence representation learning and label-specific prediction remains a critical issue for robust protein function prediction," specifically because rare labels' weak supervision signal can interfere with the shared encoder's representation learning for common labels in end-to-end multi-label frameworks — [Frontiers, S2DA-GO](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2026.1880976/full).

### Inferences
- The CAFA5 partial-knowledge finding is arguably the single most important recent structural update to the field's understanding of its own weaknesses: it reframes "novel protein function prediction" (NK/LK, historically the field's headline benchmark) as *not* the hardest real-world case — updating already-partly-known proteins is empirically harder and is the majority of real annotation growth, yet almost no reviewed system here (PLM-embedding, structure, ontology-aware, or ensemble) was purpose-built for it, since PK didn't exist as an evaluation category before CAFA5.
- Rare-term prediction and the PK problem may be related manifestations of the same underlying issue: both involve inferring more specific/nuanced functional information layered on top of what's already established (a deeper GO term given a shallower one already known, vs. any GO term for a totally blank protein), and DeepGOZero-style ontology-axiom transfer or ProTranslator-style textual transfer are plausible (though not directly tested in the sources found) candidate techniques for the PK setting specifically, since both were designed for the structurally similar "limited direct training signal, but rich indirect/relational signal available" problem.
- No source found here discussed formal, threshold-independent calibration metrics (e.g., Brier score, expected calibration error) for GO prediction scores specifically as a named field-wide open problem — this appears to be either genuinely under-studied in the literature surfaced, or requires more targeted follow-up search than budget allowed.

### Gaps
- Calibration of prediction scores specifically (as opposed to ranking/Fmax/AUPR performance) was not directly addressed by any source found in the time available — flagged explicitly as a research gap rather than inferred to be a non-issue.
- Could not fully retrieve the CAFA5 assessment paper's complete discussion (bioRxiv returned HTTP 429 rate-limit errors on three separate attempts across two URL formats; a PMC mirror was eventually used successfully, but it's possible some sections/figures/tables with additional quantitative detail on top-team rankings were not surfaced by the fetch tool's summarization).
- Did not find independent (CAFA-organizer-assessed, not self-reported) rankings/scores confirming ProtBoost's, PROTGOAT's, or GOCurator's exact final CAFA5 placement — all placement claims in this note trace back to the methods' own papers/GitHub repos, which the task's own guidance flags as a category to treat cautiously relative to independent assessment.
- Limited direct evidence located on prediction-score calibration, and none found specifically quantifying computational cost differences (e.g., wall-clock/GPU-hours) between an ensembling pipeline like GOCurator/ProtBoost versus a single end-to-end PLM classifier — relevant to a CAFA-6 technique choice but not answered here.
